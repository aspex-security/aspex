Claude Code, Cursor, Windsurf and friends reach the world through **MCP servers**: programs that give the agent your filesystem, your GitHub, your database, your browser. Most people install them by pasting a config from a README.

Every MCP security tool so far answers one question: *what could this server do?* Aspex answers five, on your machine, deterministically:

| | Command |
|---|---|
| What exists? | `aspex scan` |
| What could happen? | `aspex scan`, `aspex explain "…"` |
| What actually happened? | `aspex trace`, `aspex explore` |
| What changed? | `aspex lock`, `aspex verify`, `aspex diff main..HEAD` |
| How do I reduce risk? | `aspex tighten` |

It started from the question nobody else was answering: **what did my agents actually do?** It grew into a debugger: know what your agents can do, know what they actually did, know what changed, understand what could happen next.

## Start here

```sh
brew install aspex-security/tap/aspex && aspex     # macOS cask: brew install --cask aspex-security/tap/aspex · Node: npx aspex
```

`aspex` with no arguments reads your MCP configs and your AI clients' own logs, joins them, and shows one screen: how many tool calls your agents made in the last 30 days, how many went to servers no security scan had ever checked, how many tripped a detection rule, and the score of every configured server. Then a menu. Real output from the maintainer's machine:

```
  ▸ In the last 30 days your agents made 320 tool calls to 12 MCP servers.
  ▸ 316 of those calls went to 11 servers no security scan had ever checked.
  ▸ 2 calls tripped a detection rule. Most common: cross-server data chain (2).
  ▸ 7 configured servers, overall security score 54/100.
```

`aspex share` prints the same headlines as a privacy-safe card (counts and score only, no names or paths) you can paste anywhere. See [the front door](/tools/launcher).

Together: which risky servers are actually in use, and which servers your agents use that nothing has ever checked. `aspex-scan --with-trace` puts both in one report.

## Also included

- **`aspex-scan doctor`**: 2-second offline pre-flight for leaked secrets, over-broad filesystem grants, plaintext HTTP.
- **`aspex-attack`**: red-team a server you own with adversarial inputs. Advanced, asks for confirmation first.
- **`.aspex.yaml`** policy and finding baselines, so accepted risks are documented and CI only fails on new findings. See [Policy, baselines and prioritization](/guides/policy).

## What Aspex is not

- **Not a proxy.** It never sits in your agent's request path.
- **Not a blocker.** It shows you. You decide.
- **Not a SaaS.** Nothing to sign up for, no telemetry. The only network call is the download.
- **Not omniscient.** Connectors added through claude.ai live in the cloud and cannot be scanned statically. Aspex shows you how much of your activity runs through them instead of pretending they do not exist.

## Trust

<Note>
Aspex never phones home. Every command runs fully offline against local files. Apache-2.0, with SHA-256 checksums and an SPDX SBOM on every release. Detection is under contract: a corpus of known-malicious servers that must trigger, and popular real servers that must not, runs in CI on every change.
</Note>

Findings map to OWASP LLM Top 10 2025, MITRE ATLAS, and 20+ CWEs. [All rules](/reference/rules).
